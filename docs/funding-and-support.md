# Funding and community support

Research checked **2026-11-18** against official sources. Requirements and calls can change; reopen the linked rules before any application. These are options, not endorsements, awards, or verified eligibility. No applications, financial accounts, or commitments have been made.

**Development support** pays for software, accessibility, translation, research, or community operations. It is not a charitable donation for food assistance. OneMealForward does not claim nonprofit status, tax deductibility, or the ability to pass donations to food-relief organizations. Do not add `.github/FUNDING.yml` until an actual destination has been verified and the founder approves it.

## Options and next steps

### 1. GitHub Sponsors — potential maintainer support

Open-source contributors in supported regions can apply. The founder's actual residence, payout eligibility, and identity requirements must be confirmed by the founder; an email domain proves none of these. Review [eligibility and supported regions](https://docs.github.com/en/sponsors/getting-started-with-github-sponsors/about-github-sponsors) and [personal account setup](https://docs.github.com/en/sponsors/receiving-sponsorships-through-github-sponsors/setting-up-github-sponsors-for-your-personal-account). With explicit approval, use GitHub's Sponsors dashboard, supply the required profile and payout/tax information privately, and await approval. Never commit that information. Not active for this project; no sponsor link is published.

### 2. GitHub For Good First Issue — contributor discovery, not a grant

The [official repository](https://github.com/github/forgoodfirstissue#adding-a-new-project) requires a social-impact/civic-tech focus, SDGs in the description, setup instructions, contribution guidelines, and an update within the last month. Beginner/help-wanted labels are encouraged. Submit a PR adding the repository path in lexicographic order to `happycommits.json`, including the issues URL in the PR description. Approval belongs to their maintainers. This project targets **SDG 2: Zero Hunger**; seek a listing after launch review. No submission has been sent.

### 3. Open Source Collective — fiscal hosting, not an automatic grant

Review [eligibility](https://docs.oscollective.org/interested-in-joining-osc/acceptance-criteria), [terms](https://docs.oscollective.org/welcome-and-introduction-to-osc/terms-of-fiscal-sponsorship), and [application instructions](https://docs.oscollective.org/how-to-apply). The current software-project criteria require an organizational repository rather than a personal account, active work or usage, an open-source license, and an authorized project representative. This repository is under a personal account, so it is not ready under those standard criteria. Do not transfer ownership or create an organization without founder approval. The application checklist prefers two project administrators and offers GitHub or manual verification. [Apply here](https://opencollective.com/opensource/apply/intro) only after founder approval. [Fees](https://docs.oscollective.org/welcome-and-introduction-to-osc/fees) currently include 10% of incoming funds plus variable processor charges; there is no recurring hosting charge. This is therefore not cost-free fundraising. Founder review of eligibility, administrator responsibilities, and terms is required before applying.

### 4. Awesome Foundation Detroit — possible local pilot grant

The [Detroit chapter](https://www.awesomefoundation.org/en/chapters/detroit) lists $1,000 monthly grants and accepts individuals and informal groups. It prioritizes Detroit community benefit and excludes business startup/regular expenses and general nonprofit operations. A concrete local pilot with a specific budget may fit; a generic website maintenance request is weak. Eligibility and selection are unconfirmed. Use the chapter's **Apply For a Grant** link, describe the local activity and itemized budget, and confirm current timing and meeting expectations. Do not name an organization as a partner without consent. Obtain founder approval before submission.

### 5. NLnet — technical open-source grants, conditional fit

[Funding overview](https://nlnet.nl/funding.html) and [official proposal form](https://nlnet.nl/propose/) describe technical-development funding for open internet work, typically €5,000–€50,000 for an initial grant. Outputs must be openly licensed. Fund-specific themes and European-dimension rules apply. A general hunger volunteer website may not fit: first identify a relevant technical public-interest deliverable and matching fund. The form currently says proposals cannot be submitted and lists 2027-01-03 at noon CET as the next deadline; recheck availability before planning. Applicants must follow the current human-authorship/AI policy and write their own proposal. Do not submit this AI-produced research as a proposal. No eligibility claim or application is made.

### 6. Humanitarian innovation — later-stage research

The [WFP innovation overview](https://www.wfp.org/innovation) describes challenge-based funding, technical support, and scaling assistance. Its detailed [apply page](https://innovation.wfp.org/apply) returned 403 during research, so live calls, precise criteria, and amounts are unverified here. Revisit official calls only after a validated organizational need and a pilot; do not claim WFP endorsement.

The [UNICEF open-call page](https://www.unicef.org/innovation/open-call-applications) is **closed** and describes a frontier-technology call for for-profit startups registered in programme countries with working prototypes. This project has not demonstrated those requirements. It is not an immediate funding route; monitor future official calls rather than imply eligibility.

### 7. Free hosting and donated infrastructure

[GitHub Pages](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/what-is-github-pages) is available for public repositories on GitHub Free and is sufficient for this static, noncommercial project. No separate hosting account, database, paid API, or custom domain is necessary. [Usage restrictions](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits) still apply. Public-repository standard GitHub-hosted Actions run under GitHub's public-repository terms; do not select paid/larger runners.

[Cloudflare Pages Free limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/) list 500 builds per month, 20,000 files, and a 25 MiB per-file maximum. It is an alternative, not needed alongside current hosting; a founder-controlled account would be required. Do not enable paid products or automatic upgrades.

[TechSoup U.S. eligibility](https://support.techsoup.org/hc/en-us/articles/360055217213-How-to-Join-TechSoup-U-S) generally requires recognized public-charity status. OneMealForward does not claim that status and should not apply as a nonprofit. Provider restrictions and geography matter; donated technology is unnecessary for the current site.

## Recommended order

Launch and maintain the free static site first. Seek contributor discovery next. Consider Sponsors or fiscal hosting only with an approved, transparent development budget and the founder's willingness to manage payouts and compliance. Explore a Detroit grant only for a real local activity. Recheck this document quarterly and record changes; remove stale deadlines rather than treating them as rolling invitations.

## Founder compensation and corporate support: preparation only

GitHub Sponsors is the most direct route to investigate for individual maintainer support. Official [setup requirements](https://docs.github.com/en/sponsors/receiving-sponsorships-through-github-sponsors/setting-up-github-sponsors-for-your-personal-account) include residence in a supported region, a profile, payout/tax information and 2FA. The founder must confirm those private eligibility details and approve proceeding. Do not infer residence or account readiness from a university email. No profile, tier or payment link is activated here.

A corporate proposal should name a limited engineering scope, recipient, reviewer, budget, license, support limits and reporting method. Prepare it as an unsent draft; do not name a company as a sponsor, promise developer time, offer partner access or sell verification. Volunteer contributions are not a promise of free commercial support.

Separately contracted services may pay the founder or an agreed developer only under separately approved terms. They cannot replace an agreed free mission with an invoice. Review actual demand and capacity before setting a rate or recurring obligation. See the proposed compensation and conflict rules in [the operating plan](three-goal-operating-plan.md); they do not change current approval requirements.

The Detroit grant is a possible fit for a concrete local activity, not a dependable salary or general operating grant. Open Source Collective requires a repository/governance change before this project meets its standard software criteria. NLnet's current submission window, technical theme, European-dimension and human-authorship rules are additional barriers; it is not an immediate generic hunger-project grant. Do not claim any of these programs will fund founder compensation.

Before any application or account: approve the purpose and recipient, check current eligibility and fees, prepare an itemized budget, separate development support from aid, and decide who reviews conflicts and reports spending. No financial commitment is made by this research.
